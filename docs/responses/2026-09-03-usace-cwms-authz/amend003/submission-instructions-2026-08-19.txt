
1.0 PROPOSAL SUBMISSION REQUIREMENTS
1.1 Proposal Organization
To effectively and equitably evaluate proposals, the Government must receive information sufficiently detailed and in the order described below that clearly describe an Offeror's capabilities. It is the responsibility of the Offeror to submit complete proposal information and documents that are fully responsive to the submission requirements of the solicitation. Offerors are responsible for organizing submitted material sequentially under tabs and numbered pages to facilitate evaluation. Materials not organized under the appropriate tabs and in the sequence of subparagraphs set out below, may easily be overlooked during evaluations, possibly affecting an Offeror’s rating. Submit proposals in the format and quantities as indicated below. Proposals shall be comprised of four volumes, and submitted in electronic form, as indicated below.
Offerors shall organize their proposal into volumes as follows:
Volume I – Technical
Response to Project Technical Approach (Factor 1)
Experience, Management Approach, Key Personnel, and Transition Plan (Factor 2)
Volume II – Past Performance (Factor 3)
Volume III – Price (Factor 4)
Volume IV – Administrative Requirements
1.2 Proposal Format
Each volume shall be clearly identified and shall begin at the top of a page. All pages of each volume shall be appropriately numbered and identified by the complete company name, date, and RFP number in the header and/or footer. A Table of Contents should be created using the Table of Contents feature in MS Word.
Documents shall use the following page setup parameters:
Margins: Top, Bottom, Left, Right – 1”
Gutter: 0”
From Edge: Header, Footer 0.5”
Page Size: Width – 8.5”, Height – 11”
Spreadsheets, Tables, and Charts: May be 11” x 17” but will count as two pages. Tables and illustrations may be landscape and may use a reduced font size of no less than 10-point.
Pricing Restrictions: No Price Information is to be submitted in Volumes I, II, or IV.
The following additional restrictions apply:
File Packaging: All of the proposal files shall be compressed (zipped) into one file entitled Proposal.zip using WinZip version 6.2 or later, or as separate uploads in their native format, i.e., PDF. Please note – Self-extracting .exe files are not acceptable.
Content Requirement: The proposal shall be prepared clearly and concisely to demonstrate that the Offeror has a complete understanding of the requirements. Offerors shall prepare proposals on the presumption that the Government has no previous knowledge of the Offeror’s capabilities. Proposals will be evaluated only on the information specifically presented in respective submittals, with the exception of the Past Performance evaluations.
Cross-Referencing: When applicable, the Offeror shall cross-reference to the solicitation by annotating the solicitation reference in parentheses (e.g., Sec C, para 4.0).
Accuracy of Information: Proposals must set forth full, accurate, and complete information as required by this solicitation (including attachments). The penalty for making a false statement in proposals is prescribed in 18 U.S.C. §1001.
Award Intent: Offerors should submit their best proposal initially as the Government intends to make awards without negotiations except clarifications. The Government reserves the right to conduct negotiations, if deemed necessary.
Structure of Files: Each file of the proposal shall consist of a Table of Contents, Summary Section, and the Narrative discussion. The Summary Section shall contain a brief abstract of the file. Proprietary information must be clearly marked.

3.0 EVALUATION FACTORS AND APPROACH FOR AWARD
3.1 Evaluation Factors
The following evaluation factors will be used to evaluate each proposal:
Factor 1 – Response to Project Technical Approach: The Offeror shall be evaluated on its demonstrated technical approach to respond to PWS and Sample data. The Offeror must submit a narrative to describe its technical approach for the work described in the solicitation.
Factor 2 –Management Approach, Key Personnel, and Transition Plan: The Offeror’s Management Approach, Key Personnel, experience levels, and Transition Plan narrative will be evaluated by the Government to determine the Offeror’s capabilities to execute the work contemplated under this solicitation.
Factor 3 – Past Performance: The Offeror’s past performance will be evaluated by the Government to assess the Offeror’s probability of successfully performing the requirements in the Performance Work Statement.
Factor 4 – Price: Price will be evaluated using price analysis techniques at RFO 15.404-1, based on a comparison of competitive prices received in response to the solicitation and a comparison of proposed prices to the Independent Government Estimate (IGE). If needed, cost analysis found at RFO 15.404-2 will be used.
Award will be made to the Offeror whose proposal is most beneficial to the Government based upon an integrated assessment of the evaluation factors described below.

3.2.2.5 Evaluation Elements: The past performance factor considers each Offeror’s demonstrated recent and relevant record of performance in supplying services that meet the contract requirements. For this acquisition, recency is defined as past performance references within the last three years as of the date of this solicitation release.

3.2.3 Evaluation Criteria for Factor 4 – Price
3.2.3.1 Price Analysis and Unbalanced Pricing: Price will be evaluated using price analysis techniques at RFO 15.404-1, based on a comparison of competitive prices received in response to the solicitation and a comparison of proposed prices to the Independent Government Estimate (IGE). If needed, cost analysis found at RFO 15.404-2 will be used. The Government will evaluate prices to ensure that unbalanced pricing between Contract Line Item Numbers (CLINs) is not occurring. The proposed labor rates should reflect a clear understanding of the requirement and the ability to obtain and retain qualified personnel.

3.2.3.2 Price Reasonableness and Program Continuity: Offerors shall propose fully burdened labor rates for all labor categories. The labor rates proposed must reflect differences in skills, the complexity of various disciplines, and professional job difficulty. Proposed rates will be evaluated for reasonableness to ensure they represent a fair compensation structure capable of maintaining program continuity, uninterrupted high-quality work, and availability of required competent professional service employees. Lowered rates for essentially the same professional work should be justified to demonstrate sound management judgment and a comprehensive understanding of the requirement. Labor rates that are not in reasonable relationship to the various job categories may be viewed as evidence of failure to comprehend the complexity of the contract requirements, since it may impair the Contractor’s ability to attract and retain competent professional service employees.

4.0 PROPOSAL NEGOTIATIONS
4.1 Award Without Negotiations
In accordance with solicitation provision FAR 52.215-1, the Government intends to evaluate proposals and award a contract without negotiations with Offerors (except clarifications as described in FAR 15.306(a)). Therefore, Offerors are cautioned to provide their best terms for price and non-priced technical submission with their initial offers. The Government reserves the right to conduct negotiations, if the Contracting Officer determines that negotiations are necessary or are otherwise in the Government's best interest.

5.0 BASIS FOR AWARD
5.1 Best Value and Relative Importance
The Government will award a Firm-Fixed-Price contract to the responsible Offeror whose proposal conformed to the RFP, is fair and reasonable, and has been determined to be most beneficial to the Government based on the four evaluation factors in descending order of importance:
Factor 1 – Response to First Project Technical Approach 
Factor 2 – Management Approach, Key Personnel, and Transition Plan 
Factor 3 – Past Performance
Factor 4 – Price
The non-price factors when combined (Factors 1, 2, and 3) are significantly more important than the price factor (Factor 4). As the differences in technical and past performance ratings and relative advantages or disadvantages become less distinct, differences in prices between proposals are of increased importance in determining the most advantageous proposal. Conversely, as differences in prices become less distinct, differences in ratings and relative advantages and disadvantages between proposals are of increased importance to the determination.
To receive consideration for award, an Offeror must receive a rating of no less than acceptable for all technical factors (Factors 1 and 2) and a past performance rating of no less than neutral confidence (Factor 3).
5.2 Reservation of Rights
The Government reserves the right to accept other than the lowest price offer. The Government reserves the right to reject any and all offers.

6.0 DEFINITIONS
Clarification: Means limited exchanges between the Government and Offerors that may occur when award without negotiations is contemplated. An Offeror may be given the opportunity to clarify certain aspects of the proposals (e.g., the relevance of an Offeror’s past performance information/adverse past performance information to which the Offeror has not previously had an opportunity to respond) or to resolve minor or clerical errors. Clarification does not give the Offeror an opportunity to revise or modify its proposal, except to the extent that corrections of apparent clerical mistakes result in a revision. Clarifications do not require negotiations or submission of additional proposal information.
Communications: Exchanges between the Government and Offerors, after receipt of proposals, leading to negotiations.
Deficiency: A material failure of a proposal to meet a Government requirement or combination of significant weaknesses in a proposal that increases the risk of unsuccessful contract performance to an unacceptable level. Refer to RFO 15.001. A deficiency will preclude award action to that Offeror.
Evaluation Notice (EN): The PCO's written notification to the Offeror for purposes of clarifications, communications, or in support of negotiations.
Performance Confidence Assessment: An evaluation of the likelihood (or Government's confidence) that the Offeror will successfully perform the solicitation's requirements. The evaluation is based upon past performance information.
Recency: Pertaining to past performance information, is a measure of the time that has elapsed since the past performance reference occurred. Recency is generally expressed as a time period during which past performance references are considered relevant.
Relevancy: Pertaining to past performance information, is a measure of the extent of similarity between the service/support effort, complexity, dollar value, contract type, and subcontract/teaming or other comparable attributes of past performance examples and the source solicitation requirements; and a measure of the likelihood that the past performance is an indicator of future performance.
Requirements Documents: All aspects of the RFP that convey the needs of the Government to Offerors, including the SOO, SOW, PWS, technical requirement documents, and system requirement documents.
Risk: Pertaining to source selection, is the potential for unsuccessful contract performance. The consideration of risk assesses the degree to which an Offeror's proposed approach to achieving the technical factor may involve risk of disruption of schedule, increased cost or degradation of performance, the need for increased Government oversight, and the likelihood of unsuccessful contract performance. (For firm-fixed-price contracts, the reference to increased cost may be removed from the risk definition.)
Source Selection Authority (SSA): The official designated to make the source selection decision.
Source Selection Decision Document (SSDD): The document that reflects the SSA's independent, integrated, comparative assessment and decision.
Source Selection Evaluation Board (SSEB): A group of Government and, if needed, approved non-Government personnel, representing the various functional disciplines relevant to the acquisition.
Source Selection Plan (SSP): A plan that describes how the source selection will be organized, how proposals will be evaluated and analyzed, and how source(s) will be selected.
Strength: Any aspect of a proposal that, when judged against a stated evaluation criterion, has merit or exceeds specified performance or capability requirements in a way that will be advantageous to the Government during contract performance.
Significant Strength is an aspect of an Offeror’s proposal that has appreciable merit or appreciably exceeds specified performance or capability requirements in a way that will be appreciably advantageous to the Government during contract performance.
Subjective Trade-off: A source selection process used when it may be in the best interest of the Government to consider award to other than the lowest priced Offeror or other than the highest technically rated Offeror but is not possible to place a quantifiable value on proposed performance or capabilities above threshold (minimum) requirements.
Weakness: A flaw in the proposal that increases the risk of unsuccessful contract performance. 
Significant Weakness is a flaw that appreciably increases the risk of unsuccessful contract performance. Failure by an Offeror to rectify multiple significant weaknesses deemed essential will preclude award action to that Offeror (See RFO 15.001).
