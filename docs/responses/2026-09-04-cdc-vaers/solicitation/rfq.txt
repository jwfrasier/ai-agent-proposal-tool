     Title: Modernized Application for Consumer and Healthcare Provider VAERS Reporting


SECTION 1 – BACKGROUND
Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System (VAERS) is a national early warning system for monitoring the safety of
U.S.-licensed vaccines. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) seeks to improve VAERS reporting
for both the public and healthcare providers through a mobile-friendly, desktop-compatible web application that
increases usability, improves completeness and quality of submitted information, and reduces user burden.


This acquisition supports development of a modernized reporting experience that incorporates a redesigned VAERS
landing page and navigation, a guided reporting workflow with branching logic for public versus healthcare-
provider submitters, an intelligent validation that assists with form completion, customer centric tooltips,
informational popups and reactive design site navigation, modern / reactive popup frequently asked questions (FAQ)
features.


The solution shall protect Personally Identifiable Information (PII) and Protected Health Information (PHI) and
support integration with existing VAERS systems and processes. The Contractor shall comply with applicable
federal information security, privacy, and accessibility requirements, including HIPAA, the Privacy Act, Section
508, and FedRAMP requirements for the CDC-managed cloud environment used to process or store VAERS data.


Contractor authorization to develop and deploy on CDC infrastructure shall be governed by an Authority to Develop
(ATD) issued by CDC in coordination with the Office of the Chief Information Officer (OCIO).

SUBSECTION A – DEFINITIONS
•   ATD (Authority to Develop): Authorization issued by CDC, coordinated with OCIO, permitting the
    Contractor to develop, locally test, update, and deploy the application on CDC infrastructure following review
    of adequate security and privacy documentation.
•   ATO (Authority to Operate): is an official management decision by a designated federal authorizing official
    that permits an information system to operate and explicitly accepts the risk to organizational operations, assets,
    individuals, other organizations, and the Nation based on implemented security and privacy controls
•   Branching Logic: Conditional form flow that presents or suppresses data fields based on submitter type (public
    vs. healthcare provider) and report characteristics (e.g., vaccine error with no adverse event).
•   EPLC: Enterprise Performance LifeCycle, comprised of Initiation, Concept, Planning, Requirements Analysis,
    Design, Development, Test, Implementation, Operations & Maintenance, and Disposition where applicable.
•   FedRAMP: Federal Risk and Authorization Management Program.
•   FISMA: Federal Information Security Modernization Act (updated in 2014).
•   FITARA: Federal Information Technology Acquisition Reform Act.
•   HIPAA: Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act of 1996, as amended, including applicable
    Privacy and Security Rules.
•   Low-Code / Configurable UI: Application design that enables authorized CDC program personnel to make
    content and interface updates with limited developer support.
•   OCIO: Office of the Chief Information Officer (CDC).
•   PHI: Protected Health Information.
•   PII: Personally Identifiable Information.
•   Section 508: Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act of 1973, as amended, and applicable HHS ICT accessibility
    requirements.
•   VAERS: Vaccine Adverse Event Reporting System.

SECTION 2 – PURPOSE/OBJECTIVE
CDC requires design, development, and deployment of a modernized, mobile-friendly, desktop-compatible web
application to support both public and healthcare-provider submission of VAERS reports. The purpose of this
acquisition is to:

    •   Simplify and streamline the reporting experience for both public and healthcare-provider submitters while
        improving completeness and accuracy of submitted data.
    •   Redesign the VAERS landing page and navigation menus to provide clearer access to reporting tools,
        FAQs, and data downloads.
    •   Provide a branching-logic reporting workflow that tailors data fields to submitter type and report
        characteristics, using the data elements of the current VAERS form.
    •   Provide intelligent completion assistance, answers user questions, and supports site navigation, with
        tooltips and frequently asked questions
    •   Provide an integrated medical record upload capability while maintaining the existing post-submission
        upload tool.
    •   Enable authorized CDC program personnel to make interface and content updates with limited developer
        support, using current, state-of-the-art UI/UX design principles.
    •   Ensure security, privacy, and regulatory compliance for all data processed, including HIPAA and Privacy
    •   Act protections, FISMA and FedRAMP requirements, and Section 508 accessibility, and support issuance
        of an ATD.
    •   Produce VAERS-compatible data outputs and support integration with existing VAERS systems and
        processes.
SECTION 3 – SCOPE OF WORK
Within the period of performance, the Contractor shall design, develop, test, and deliver a responsive, mobile-
friendly, desktop-compatible web application that enables both the public and healthcare providers to submit
VAERS reports with improved usability and data quality.


The scope includes human-centered user experience (UX) design; redesign of the VAERS landing page and
navigation menus for clearer access to reporting tools, FAQs, and data downloads; front-end and back-end
application development; a branching-logic report submission form supporting public and healthcare-provider
submitters using the data elements of the current VAERS form; implementation intelligent validation to help users
through report completion, answer questions, and assist with site navigation; an integrated follow-up information
upload capability; a low-code/configurable interface enabling authorized CDC program personnel to make updates
with limited developer support; and production of structured data compatible with VAERS requirements to support
integration with existing VAERS systems and workflows, operating within the CDC-managed hosting environment
in MS Azure.


The application shall employ current, state-of-the-art UI/UX design principles.


The Contractor shall coordinate with the other existing contracts supporting the VAERS system and CDC/OCIO
hosting teams to stand up the necessary environment within CDC’s cloud infrastructure, develop, and deploy the
application after appropriate approvals, including issuance of an ATD.


The Contractor shall implement, document, and obtain approval for required governance stage gates, security,
privacy, and accessibility controls for all data processed, including HIPAA and Privacy Act protections, FISMA and
FedRAMP requirements for the CDC-managed cloud environment, and Section 508 conformance for the application
and all contract deliverables.


CDC will provide coordination, feedback, VAERS integration requirements, and access to the CDC Azure
environment as applicable.


SECTION 4 – TASKS TO BE PERFORMED

Task 1: Design and Develop Mobile-Friendly, Desktop-Compatible Web
Application

1.1 The Contractor shall collaborate with CDC stakeholders to confirm functional requirements, VAERS data
elements, integration constraints, and success metrics for both public and healthcare-provider reporting.


1.2 The Contractor shall produce UX artifacts (e.g., user flows, wireframes, and clickable prototypes) and
incorporate CDC feedback prior to development, applying current, state-of-the-art UI/UX design principles.
1.3 The Contractor shall design and develop a responsive web application optimized for mobile devices and modern
desktop browsers, enabling users to access and complete reporting from any device with internet access, including
form validation, error handling, and user-friendly data entry to reduce reporting burden.


1.4 The Contractor shall redesign the VAERS landing page and navigation menus to provide clearer access to
reporting tools, FAQs, and data downloads, including a link to the simplified report submission form (Task 1.6).


1.5 The Contractor shall implement a customer satisfaction survey for navigating the VAERS site.


1.6 The Contractor shall develop a mobile-friendly, desktop-compatible report submission form that uses branching
logic to serve both public and healthcare-provider submitters using the data elements of the current VAERS form.
The Contractor shall:

    •    1.6.1 Substitute simpler, plain-language text for the public version that requires less health expertise.
    •    1.6.2 Implement branching logic so that healthcare providers submitting a vaccine error report with no
         adverse event (AE) are not required to complete AE-related fields.

1.7 The Contractor shall implement a pop-up customer satisfaction survey presented upon VAERS submission to
measure satisfaction with the submission process.


1.8 The Contractor shall implement a low-code/configurable interface enabling authorized CDC program personnel
to make content and interface updates with limited developer support, and shall document the change and
configuration process.


1.9 The Contractor shall implement VAERS-compatible data capture and ability to transmit data (e.g., structured
data mappings) to support integration with existing VAERS systems and workflows, as directed by CDC.


1.10 The Contractor shall implement performance optimization to achieve page load time of ≤ 3 seconds under
normal operating conditions, support a median end-to-end submission time of ≤ 10 minutes for typical reports, and
reduce the submission abandonment rate by ≥ 30% compared to the baseline established at project kickoff.


1.11 The Contractor shall deliver working builds for Government review and conduct iterative defect remediation
through beta and final release.


1.12 The Contractor shall develop user-facing documentation and support materials aligned with the final
application user experience.



Task 2: Implement Medical Record Upload and Document Management

2.1 The Contractor shall integrate an upload tool for medical record information as part of the form update, while
maintaining the current upload tool for documents that become available post-submission.
2.2 The Contractor shall include a free-text box allowing submitters to provide additional information as free text.


2.3 The Contractor shall implement a tool that scans healthcare-provider submissions and suggests supplemental
documents to upload.


2.4 The Contractor shall implement a two-phase approach to uploaded documents:

    •    2.4.1 Phase 1 (base scope): Restrict accepted documents in the upload tool to medical records and vaccine
         documents related to vaccine administration or the adverse event reported.
    •    2.4.2 Phase 2 (optional / future phase, not in base scope): Following Government evaluation of Phase 1,
         and if exercised as a future option or contract modification, allow healthcare providers to upload pictures
         and medical images related to the adverse event reported. The Contractor shall design Phase 1 to
         accommodate future Phase 2 expansion.

2.5 The Contractor shall ensure all upload and document-management functionality complies with security, privacy,
and accessibility requirements for handling PHI/PII.


2.6 The Contractor shall remediate defects and optimize upload performance through beta and final release.



Task 3: Ensure Security, Privacy, FISMA, FedRAMP, HIPAA, Section 508
Compliance, and Support ATD Issuance

3.1 The Contractor shall design, develop, and document the application in a manner that protects PHI and PII and
supports compliance with HIPAA and the Privacy Act for all data collected, processed, transmitted, and stored.


3.2 The Contractor shall ensure the application is developed and deployed within the CDC-managed FedRAMP-
authorized Azure environment approved for use by CDC, consistent with Government security requirements.


3.3 The Contractor shall prepare and submit adequate security and privacy documentation sufficient for CDC, in
coordination with OCIO, to issue an Authority to Develop (ATD) enabling local testing, updates, and product
deployment on CDC infrastructure. The Contractor shall address Government review comments and remediate
identified weaknesses as a condition of ATD issuance.


3.4 The Contractor shall implement and document appropriate technical and administrative safeguards (e.g., access
controls, encryption in transit and at rest, audit logging, and secure configuration) consistent with CDC direction.
The Contractor shall perform application vulnerability scanning during development and before deployment to
eliminate known vulnerabilities.


3.5 The Contractor shall ensure that the web application and all deliverables (including documentation) conform to
Section 508 and applicable HHS ICT accessibility standards and shall provide an Accessibility Conformance Report
(ACR/VPAT) and remediation plan for any nonconformities.
3.6 The Contractor shall prepare and submit required security, privacy, and compliance documentation identified in
Section 8, and shall address Government review comments and identified weaknesses.


3.7 The Contractor shall conduct functional, security, and accessibility testing and remediate identified defects prior
to beta and final release.


3.8 The Contractor shall provide user guides and administrator/technical guides sufficient to support CDC operation,
maintenance, and future enhancements, including guidance for program-personnel configuration (Task 1.8).



Task 4 – Transition Out

Preparations for Transition-Out should be started by the contractor in conjunction with the COR and
technical/programmatic government staff early in the contract execution, developing a state of readiness for
transition to both government staff and subsequent contractors. Materials should include links to all code
repositories, accounts, rights, and necessary SOPs to continue execution of the functions.

The Contractor shall:
4.1 Maintain a Transition-out readiness posture where documentation is assessed at least monthly based on project
delivery timeframes and provided to the COR and technical representatives. This should include (but is not limited
to) any documentation, system and email and other account access, code bases, processes, and other assets necessary
to understand and operate the systems and related procedures. These assets and the ability to assign access should
always be available to designated FTEs to ensure continuity of operations.

4.2 Facilitate the transition out of contracted activities and services to the Federal Government or, to a follow-on
Contractor by the end of the task order period of performance.

4.3 During the transition-out, ensure no degradation in support provided under the Task Order.

4.4 The incumbent Contractor shall submit to the COR, for approval, a detailed Transition-out Plan at least two (2)
months prior to contract end. The plan shall identify tasks, roles, and responsibilities for contractor staff during the
transition, and transition-out schedule and milestones to ensure an orderly and complete transition.

4.5 Lead Technical Exchange Meetings (TEMs) with the successor to impart knowledge transfer of all processes,
projects, systems, applications, and databases, as well as sustainment activities.

4.6 Provide the government with the accurate and most current versions of all documentation developed under the
TO.

4.7 Provide the government a current inventory of all Government-owned assets used by the Contractor along with
full support in the reconciliation of this inventory.

4.8 Provide the government with access to full documentation of and all custom code, user accounts, and
configurations (not COTS source code), reports, process automations, scripts and configurations with applicable
configuration management information.

4.9 Provide current status and reference to materials for governance, security and privacy or other approval aspects
of each project or activity.

4.10 Provide the successor with the ability to “shadow” and participate in other knowledge transfer meetings and
opportunities to facilitate the transfer of information, processes, and data needed to continue the services being
performed by the Contractor
4.11 Perform all task order closeout activities and provide sustainment support to complete the transition.

Information Security and Privacy

The Contractor shall comply with all applicable CDC, HHS, and federal information security and privacy
requirements, including protections for PII and PHI under the Privacy Act and HIPAA. The Contractor shall
implement security and privacy-by-design practices, including secure handling of PHI/PII during collection,
processing, transmission, and storage, and shall use the CDC Azure environment (FedRAMP authorized and
approved for use by CDC) to integrate with existing VAERS infrastructure. The Contractor shall provide adequate
security and privacy documentation sufficient for CDC, in coordination with OCIO, to issue an Authority to
Develop (ATD) to deploy and Authority to Operate (ATO) the application onCDC infrastructure and enable local
testing, updates, and deployment, and shall support Government reviews by responding to findings and
implementing approved remediation.

d.   Privacy Compliance. Comply with the E-Government Act of 2002, NIST SP 800-53, and applicable
     CDC privacy policies, and complete all the requirements below:
       i.   Per the Office of Management and Budget (OMB) Circular A-130, Personally Identifiable
            Information (PII), is "information that can be used to distinguish or trace an individual's identity,
            either alone or when combined with other information that is linked or linkable to a specific
            individual." Examples of PII include, but are not limited to the following: Social Security
            number, date and place of birth, mother's maiden name, biometric records, etc.
      ii.   To ensure that the public's personal information is protected in a manner commensurate with the
            privacy risks, CDC uses a privacy analysis process to assess the risks associated with CDC's
            collection and maintenance of PII and to ensure information is handled in accordance with
            applicable legal, regulatory, and policy requirements. PTAs analyze how information is handled
            in IT systems and electronic information collections and determines if the IT system or
            electronic information collection collects, disseminates, maintains, or disposes of PII. PIAs are
            used to assess the privacy risks of IT systems and electronic information collections that collect,
            disseminate, maintain, or dispose of PII about members of the public. PIAs also provide
            transparency into how CDC collects, disseminates, maintains, or disposes of the public's PII.

                determined by the agency that a review is required based on a major change to the system,
                or when new types of PII are collected that introduces new or increased privacy risks,
                whichever comes first.

    1. The plan must describe how Government data, outputs, prompts, logs, and outputs be accessed,

CDCL.10 Data Management Plan (Instructions) (NOV 2018)

CDC requires awardees of contracts that involve the collection or generation of public health data with
federal funds to develop, submit, and comply with the requirement of a Data Management Plan (DMP)
for each collection or generation of public health data undertaken. Consistent with the terms of and
activities expected under the statement of work, the offeror shall develop and submit a DMP as part of its
proposal in response to this solicitation, and if awarded a contract, update the DMP throughout the life
cycle of the public health data collected or generated during the performance period. The DMP shall
describe, to the extent appropriate, the data to be collected or generated; plans for making the data
accessible that state what the data represent and potential limitations for use, provisions for the protection
of privacy, confidentiality, security, intellectual property, or other rights (where these protections preclude
making data accessible, justification must be provided); and plans for archiving and long-term
preservation of the data. Costs, if any, associated with developing and implementing a DMP, including
costs of sharing, archiving and long-term preservation, are allowable costs and shall therefore be reflected
in an offeror’s Business Proposal. A DMP must be developed in accordance with the below guidelines.

A proposal received without a DMP will be deemed “Unacceptable”.

A DMP for each collection and/or generation of public health data should include the following
information:
    • A description of the public health data to be collected or generated in the contract period of
       performance;
    • Standards to be used for the collected or generated public health data;
    •   Mechanisms for or limitations to providing access to and sharing of the data (include a
        description of provisions for the protection of privacy, confidentiality, security, intellectual
        property, or other rights) or justification for why data cannot be made accessible This section
        should address access to identifiable and de-identified data (see below for additional information
        about access);
    •   Statement of the use of data standards that ensure all released data have appropriate
        documentation that describes the method of collection, what the data represent, and potential
        limitations for use; and;
    •   Plans for archiving and long-term preservation of the data, or explanation of why long-term
        preservation and access are not justified. This section should address archiving and preservation
        of identifiable and de-identified data (see Public Access to CDC Funded Digital Public Health
        Data) for additional information regarding archiving).

                          Section G – Basis of Award and Evaluation Factors


1. Basis of Award – Best Value Tradeoff Source Selection

This contract shall be awarded using a best value tradeoff source selection process. Award will be made
to the responsible Offeror whose proposal is determined, in accordance with the evaluation criteria, to
represent the best overall value and be most advantageous to the Government.

The Government will evaluate proposals based on the stated non-price evaluation factors and price,
considering the relative strengths, weaknesses, risks, and overall value presented by each Offeror. The
Government will assess the extent to which each proposal demonstrates a clear understanding of the
requirements, a sound and feasible technical approach, relevant experience, qualified resources, effective
management, and a reasonable likelihood of successful contract performance.

Trade-offs between price and non-price factors are expressly permitted. The Government may determine
that a proposal offering superior technical capability, reduced performance risk, stronger relevant
experience, greater management effectiveness, or other meaningful advantages provides greater overall
value than a lower-priced proposal. Accordingly, the Government expressly reserves the right to award to
an Offeror other than the Offeror submitting the lowest-priced proposal. However, the Government will
not pay a significantly higher overall price solely to obtain minor, incremental, or otherwise marginal
improvements in performance that do not provide a commensurate benefit to the Government.

The Government's source selection determination will be based solely on the information contained in the
submitted proposals and the evaluation criteria established in this solicitation, subject to any additional
information the Government is authorized to consider under applicable acquisition regulations. Offerors
shall therefore ensure that the proposals clearly identify and substantiate the specific benefits,
discriminators, capabilities, and advantages they are offering.

The Government reserves the right to make award based on the initial proposals without conducting
negotiations or requesting proposal revisions. Offerors should therefore submit the most advantageous
Technical and Price Proposals initially and should not assume that an opportunity will be provided to
correct deficiencies, enhance strengths, or revise pricing after submission.

3. Price Proposal Evaluation

The Price Proposal will be evaluated for accuracy, completeness, internal consistency, and overall price
reasonableness. The Offeror bears the responsibility for demonstrating that its proposed price is fair,
reasonable, and adequately supported. Proposed pricing shall establish a logical relationship among all
cost and price elements, the proposed technical approach, level of effort, labor categories and hours,
resources, deliverables, and other requirements. The Government will assess whether the Price Proposal
reflects a clear understanding of the requirement and is consistent with the Offeror’s Technical Proposal.

The Government may evaluate the proposed price for completeness and consistency and may consider
any significant omissions, unsupported assumptions, mathematical discrepancies, or inconsistencies
between the Technical and Price Proposals. The Government may request additional supporting
information, as permitted by the solicitation and applicable regulations, to determine the basis and
reasonableness of proposed prices.

To determine fair and reasonable pricing, the Government may use any appropriate price analysis
techniques and procedures, including comparison of proposed prices received, historical prices paid by
the Government or other customers, parametric estimating methods, competitive or published price lists,
the Independent Government Cost Estimate (IGCE), market research for the same or similar
requirements, and comparison of individual cost elements such as labor, materials, travel, and other direct
costs.


4. Past Performance Evaluation

The Government shall obtain past performance information on the Offeror. The past performance
information can be obtained from a variety of sources including, but not limited to, information reported in
CPARS, feedback obtained from points of contact on existing CDC contracts, and feedback obtained from
points of contact on other federal or commercial contracts.

Each Offeror shall be evaluated on its past performance under current and prior contracts. By past
performance, the Government means the Offeror’s record of conforming to specifications and to standards
of good workmanship, the Offeror’s record of forecasting and controlling costs, the Offeror’s adherence to
contract schedules and terms, including the administrative aspects of performance, the Offeror’s reputation
for reasonable and cooperative behavior and commitment to customer satisfaction, and generally, the
Offeror’s business-like concern for the interest of the customer and the degree of quality of deliverables
and performance. If CPARS is used, CPARS standard rating areas include quality, schedule, cost,
management, small business, and regulatory compliance.

Each Offeror shall be evaluated as positive, negative, or neutral on its past performance under current and
prior contracts. An Offeror without a record of past performance or for whom past performance is not
available shall be evaluated as neutral.
